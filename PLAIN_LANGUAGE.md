# FairMarket in Plain Words

*A two-minute version of the idea, for anyone who has ever paid too much on a delivery app.*

## Why does my $20 meal cost $30 on the app?

It is not the restaurant's fault. Here is what actually happens:

1. The delivery app charges the restaurant about 30 percent of every order.
2. The restaurant cannot afford to lose 30 percent, so it raises its prices on the app. The $20 dish becomes $24.
3. Then the app adds its own fees on top: a service fee, a delivery fee.
4. You pay $30 for a $20 meal. The restaurant gets about $17.

Everyone in this chain is behaving reasonably. The problem is the structure: the app sits in the middle and takes a cut big enough to distort every price around it.

## This is everywhere, not just food

Ride hailing takes 30 to 45 percent of the fare. Amazon takes 30 to 35 percent from sellers once all fees combine. Same pattern: the platform taxes the transaction, the seller raises the price, the buyer pays.

## The fix

Separate the marketplace from the app, the way India separated payments from payment apps with UPI. One neutral set of rules that any app can use:

- **The restaurant lists its real price.** $20 means $20, on every app, always.
- **You pay one small, visible fee.** Instead of a hidden 30 percent baked into inflated prices, there is a single fee of at most 8 percent, shown as its own line on your bill.
- **Delivery is priced honestly.** The driver still needs to be paid, so delivery shows up as its own transparent line instead of being buried in markups.
- **The restaurant keeps almost everything.** $20 out of your $20, instead of $17.

The same $20 meal: $20 for the food, $1.60 fee, $3.50 delivery. About $25 instead of $30. The restaurant earns more. You pay less. The app still earns its fee. The only loser is the extraction.

## What this means for you

**If you run a restaurant or sell anything online:** you list once, at your real price, and it appears on every app that follows the rules. Your ratings and your customers move with you. No app can hold them hostage or punish you for selling elsewhere.

**If you buy:** you see the real price and every fee before you pay. No surprises at checkout.

**If you drive or deliver:** delivery is a separate, visible line item, not a cost squeezed out of someone else's margin.
